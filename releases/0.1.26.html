Volt 0.1.26

Reverts the forced rotation added in 0.1.25. It was justified by a theory
that testing disproved, and it makes things worse rather than merely being
inert.

The claim was that OpenAI revokes the refresh token minted by the previous
codex login on a machine, so rotating onto a descendant would escape it.
Neither half held up. A login does not revoke earlier grants -- three
sessions authenticated on different days, two of them for the same
account, coexist happily on one installation. And a credential that was
rotated exactly as intended was invalidated anyway.

What actually breaks a credential is two clients sharing one refresh
chain. A refresh token may only be redeemed once, and Volt holds the same
one as ~/.codex immediately after an import. Rotating on import spends
Codex's on-disk copy right away, so the CLI presents a used token on its
next refresh and reuse detection can revoke the whole family -- taking
Volt and the CLI down together. Refreshing only near expiry leaves that
copy usable. The real remedy is procedural: re-run codex login after
importing so the two clients stop sharing a session.

The 401/403 split from 0.1.25 is unaffected and stays. Both revocations
observed returned 401, confirming that mapping; 403 remains a transport
condition that must not be reported as rejected credentials.

The refresh-threshold tests are kept and reframed around the constraint
that actually motivates the narrow window.
